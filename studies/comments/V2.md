# Comments v2 — the panel, and the replication at scale

_Pre-registered 2026-09-19, before the first collection day. Amendments go under
`## Amendments` with a date; nothing above that line changes after data arrives._

v1 (`README.md`, published 2026-08-16) took one snapshot per video after the fact and
found exposure — post time plus video scale — explains 83.5% of the above-chance
within-video ordering of likes. One snapshot cannot see the ranking *form*. v2 watches
it form, and re-runs v1's estimate on a corpus seven times larger across three
languages.

Two studies, one collector, one key. Code: `panel.py` (new), `collect.py` (v1,
unchanged). Driver: `run-daily.sh` under `dev.ai-arena.comments-daily` at 01:00
Pacific (right after the quota reset) and 13:00.

## Study A — the panel

### Question

When is a comment's fate decided? Concretely: how many days after upload does the
displayed top page stop changing, and were the comments that end up there posted in
the first hours?

### Design

- **Enrollment.** Each day, every configured channel's uploads playlist is read once
  (1 unit). Videos published in the previous ~30 hours are enrolled, interleaved across
  languages, up to 80 a day, until the panel holds **500 videos**. Expected enrollment
  period: 6–8 days.
- **Observation.** Every active video, **twice a day** (periods `<day>a` before noon
  Pacific, `<day>b` after) for **21 days** from enrollment: its `order=relevance` first
  page — up to 100 comments with rank, like count, reply count, post time, text — and
  its view/like/comment totals. One unit per video per period for the page, one per 50
  videos for totals. Twice, not once, because a video enrolled at 01:00 is anywhere
  from 1 to 30 hours old, and if the page locks in within days the movement is in the
  first 48 hours; one point a day cannot resolve it. **Analysis is in hours since
  upload at fetch time, never in period index.**
- **Reliability floor.** Each run re-fetches 20 random pages it just fetched (period
  `<period>r`). v1 documented that `order=relevance` is nondeterministic across calls;
  the overlap between a page and its immediate repeat is the noise floor, and no
  "the ranking moved" claim is made below it. Report the floor (median top-10 and
  top-20 Jaccard between pairs) before any lock-in number.
- **Per-channel caps.** At most 2 enrollments per channel per day and 5 over the whole
  panel, so a daily Shorts poster cannot supply a fifth of the videos. Standard errors
  are clustered by channel in every analysis.
- **Population.** 332 channels in `config/panel-channels.json`: 166 English (v1's 38,
  three handles corrected, plus 128), 83 Chinese (Taiwan, Hong Kong, overseas
  Mandarin/Cantonese — the mainland is not on YouTube, so this is *Chinese-language
  YouTube*, not the Chinese internet), 83 Japanese. Genre-mixed within language; ~12%
  Shorts-heavy per language. Subscriber range roughly 300K–15M so videos land in the
  200–20,000-comment band v1 used. Compiled by hand from list pages and verified
  handle-by-handle against youtube.com on 2026-09-19.
  - This is a **stratified convenience sample**, as v1's was. It generalizes to
    established creator channels with active comment sections — not to YouTube, not to
    small channels, corporate media, or anything with comments off.
  - Enrollment interleaves languages, so the panel lands near 167 videos per language
    despite en having twice the channels. Languages compare on equal n.
  - Genre is a covariate, not a balanced stratum: zh skews entertainment/commentary, ja
    entertainment/gaming/music, because that is where those markets' mid-to-large
    creators are. Report per genre; do not force parity.
  - v1's 38 English channels stay in as the anchor, so the English replication is a
    true replication (same channels, new window) plus an extension (new channels).
  - `panel status` flags channels with no upload in 30 days; a quiet channel is
    reported, not silently carried. Unresolved handles are reported the same way.
- **What is not observed.** Comments outside the displayed first page. The panel sees
  who is on the page, not who is off it; the bulk study (B) supplies the off-page
  distribution. Pinned and creator-hearted status are not exposed by the API and are not
  a variable here. **Creator comments** (`author_channel_id == channel_id`) are
  derivable and are excluded from lock-in sets — they are usually pinned and sit in a
  different regime. **The page observed is the anonymous API ranking**, presumed equal
  to the logged-out default "Top comments" view; spot-check five panel videos
  logged-out against the API page on day 3 and record the match in Amendments.

### Endpoints

**Inclusion rule for lock-in analyses:** ≥ 100 top-level comments at the endpoint
(≈ `commentCount × 0.7`, v1's measured thread share). A 30-comment video has a stable
top-10 by construction. Report how many videos the rule drops, by language.

Primary endpoint is **day 7** (amended day 0, before any lock-in data existed — see
Amendments), so every enrolled video has one by collection day ~14. Day 14 and day 20
are secondary endpoints, reported in the update on the cohorts that reach them. If the
day-7 page is still drifting (H1 fails at 72 h and the overlap curve has not flattened),
the post waits for day 14 — a day-7 endpoint cannot tell "locked" from "slow".

1. **Lock-in day.** For each video, the first day *d* at which the top-10 set has
   Jaccard overlap ≥ 0.8 with the day-7 top-10. Report the median and IQR, by language
   and by format (Shorts ≤ 60 s vs long-form).
2. **Persistence curve.** Mean top-20 Jaccard overlap between period *t* and day 7, over
   hours since upload; and Spearman rank correlation of the period-*t* vs day-7 page over the
   comments present on both.
3. **Entry age.** For every comment on the day-7 top-20, its age at posting (comment
   `published_at` − video `published_at`). Report the share posted within the first
   hour, first 6 hours, first 24 hours.
4. **Late entrants.** Comments that first appear in the top-20 on day ≥ 3: count per
   video, and their posting age — are they late *posts* or late *risers*?
5. **The feedback loop, directly.** Per-period like gain for comments on the displayed
   page as a function of their rank in the previous period. Identified **within
   comment**: the same comment's gain in periods it sat at rank ≤ 10 versus periods it
   sat at > 50, so quality is held fixed and only position varies. Comments that never
   move contribute nothing to this estimate. Still observational — what moves a comment
   is not random — and the write-up says so. v1 estimated this circularity indirectly
   (`circularity.py`); here it is a difference in observed increments.

### Hypotheses (numbers first, so a miss is a miss)

- **H1.** Median lock-in ≤ 72 hours after upload, and the lock-in overlap threshold
  (0.8) sits clearly above the reliability floor. (Kill: > 7 days means the page keeps
  moving for a week and "decided early" is the wrong story; a floor near 0.8 means the
  API is too noisy to say anything at top-10 and the analysis moves to top-20.)
- **H2.** ≥ 70% of day-7 top-20 comments were posted within the first 24 hours of the
  video; ≥ 30% within the first hour.
- **H3.** Within comment, a period at rank 1–10 gains at least 3× the likes of a period
  at rank 51–100.
- **H4.** H1 and H2 hold in each language within ±1 day and ±10 points of the pooled
  value. A language that departs by more is the finding, not a nuisance.
- **H5.** Shorts lock in faster than long-form (median lock-in day at least 1 lower),
  consistent with v1's 7.6× early-decile ratio for Shorts.

### Kill / promote gate

- Fewer than **200 videos with ≥ 7 observation days** → pilot, not a result. Report it
  as such and extend rather than publish.
- Enrollment stalls under 40/day for three days → the channel list is too quiet; widen
  it (amendment), don't lower the target silently.
- Any period with > 20% of active videos unobserved for budget reasons is a hole in
  the series; note it in the manifest. Two in a row → raise `PANEL_BUDGET`.

### Cost

~330 units/day discovery during enrollment, ~1,000/day observation at full panel (two
periods), ~40/day reliability repeats, ~20/day totals. Budget `PANEL_BUDGET=2000` per
day. The whole panel is ~35k units over four weeks.

## Study B — replication at 10M, three languages

### Question

Does v1's result — exposure explains most of the above-chance ordering of likes within a
video — hold across languages and at an order of magnitude more data, or was 83.5% a
property of 38 English channels?

### Design

v1's collector unchanged (`collect.py`: frozen population, `order=time` full
enumeration, one `relevance` page per video, smallest-first queue). Population frozen
from the same ~300 channels' back catalogue, window 2025-09-01..2026-08-31, 200–20,000
comments per video, up to 40 videos per channel; target ~7,000 videos / ~10M top-level
comments. `population.freeze` reports unresolved handles and excluded videos in the
manifest; check both before the first run.

**Amend before freezing if the estimated units exceed 130k** — at `BULK_BUDGET=7000`
that is 19 collection days, and the post should not wait on it. Prefer fewer videos per
channel over fewer channels.

### Endpoints

v1's pre-registered metric (`README.md` "The exact metric"), computed per language and
pooled, plus the zero-like share and top-1% like share per language.

### Hypotheses

- **H6.** Exposure share ≥ 70% in each language. (v1: 83.5% pooled English.)
- **H7.** Zero-like share is within 75–90% in each language.
- **H8.** The content-only model's AUC stays below 0.60 in each language — text still
  does not predict likes once exposure is out.

### Cost

~100k units for comments plus ~2k for freeze and rank pages. At 7,000/day: 14–15 days.
Runs concurrently with the panel on the same key: the day is split 2,000 / 7,000 with
1,000 headroom for the API's own count.

## Key and quota

v1 ran on `channel-engine`'s key and its README named "scaling past the pilot" as the
trigger for a second project. This is that. v2 runs on its own Google Cloud project's
key, in `ai-arena/.env` as `YOUTUBE_API_KEY`, sourced by `run-daily.sh` and never
printed. Quota is 10,000 units/day per project, reset midnight Pacific.

## Schedule

| Day | What |
|---|---|
| 0 (first run) | panel init + first enrollment; population freeze for B; first bulk day |
| ~7 | panel full (500); enrollment stops |
| ~15 | bulk collection complete |
| ~14 | every panel video has its day-7 endpoint → analysis, then the post (~Oct 3) |
| ~22 | every video has day 14 → first update, with the bulk replication |
| ~28 | last cohort reaches day 20 → secondary endpoints, dataset release |

The post is a one-number measurement — *the day your comment's fate is decided* — in
the shape of `comment-likes-are-timing`, with the three-language replication as its
second half.
